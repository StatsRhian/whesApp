# Data Explorer

**Overview**

[Monitoring health inequalities is essential for achieving health equity](https://www.who.int/data/health-equity/health-equity-monitor-database). The Data Explorer brings together data from a range of national data sources and is categorised by essential condition. Data regarding the health and wellbeing impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic are also included in the Data Explorer and are derived from the Public Health Wales [public engagement survey](https://phwwhocc.co.uk/resources/?filter-keywords=public+engagement+survey&filter-year=&filter-team=&filter-type=). 

The data within the Data Explorer are disaggregated – or grouped – according to sex, age, deprivation quintile and, for some indicators, education. [Disaggregated data](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4576419/) provide [data on who is being left behind](https://www.who.int/data/health-equity/health-equity-monitor-database); they break down national averages, helping to identify patterns of how indicators of health are distributed throughout a population. In doing so, disaggregated data can help to identify disadvantaged subgroups within populations and, in turn, inform equity-oriented health policies, programs, and practices. At present, the Data Explorer does not contain indicators that can be broken-down to [protected characteristics](https://www.equalityhumanrights.com/en/equality-act/protected-characteristics); the ambition is to integrate these in time when the data is available. 

Notwithstanding the [persistent gaps and fragmentation in public health data and the need to invest in strong health information systems](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/in-focus-2021), data is beginning to emerge that sheds light on the protected characteristics and inequalities. This includes the Welsh Government [analysis of protected characteristics by area deprivation](https://gov.wales/analysis-protected-characteristics-area-deprivation-2017-2019), and a series of datasets and interactive tools on [COVID-19, employment and the protected characteristics](https://gov.wales/coronavirus-and-employment-analysis-protected-characteristics).

The Government Statistical Service provides [UK equalities data](https://gss.civilservice.gov.uk/dashboard/tools/equalities-statistics/database.html#about-the-database) via a statistics database that includes data on the protected characteristics, including data for Wales. Also, the Office for National Statistics [Centre for Equalities and Inclusion](https://www.ons.gov.uk/aboutus/whatwedo/programmesandprojects/onscentres/centreforequalitiesandinclusion) supports the UK [Equality Data Programme](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/new-large-scale-data-project-will-get-to-the-heart-of-disparities) to better understand the barriers people face across the UK, and also provides data and evidence to explore how protected characteristic groups and others at risk of disadvantage are affected by current social and policy issues and how multiple characteristics can come together – or intersect - to shape people's experiences.

### Useful resources and data portals:

[Public Health Wales Observatory](https://public.tableau.com/app/profile/publichealthwalesobservatory) providing data analysis, evidence finding and knowledge management to support decision makers and the public with health information.

**Welsh Government** [StatsWales](https://statswales.gov.wales/Catalogue) providing a free-to-use service to view, manipulate, create and download tables from Welsh data including information on Wales’ population, economy, government spending and performance as well as the environment, education, transport and health.

**Welsh Government** [Survey for Wales](https://gov.wales/national-survey-wales) involving around 12,000 people each year, with results informing the Welsh Government to help make Wales a better place to live.

**Secure Anonymised Information Linkage** [(SAIL) Databank](https://saildatabank.com/) for secure storage and use of anonymised person-based data for research to improve health, well-being and services.

[Data Cymru](https://www.data.cymru/eng/home) putting data and intelligence at the heart of public service delivery.

**Office for National Statistics** [Centre for Equalities and Inclusion](https://www.ons.gov.uk/aboutus/whatwedo/programmesandprojects/onscentres/centreforequalitiesandinclusion) aiming to improve the evidence base for understanding equity and fairness in the UK today, enabling new insights into important policy questions.  

**Office for National Statistics** [Measuring equality](https://www.ons.gov.uk/methodology/classificationsandstandards/measuringequality) including resources and guidance on data on ethnic group, national identity, religion, and sexual identity; and an overview of our work on gender identity. 

**The Health Foundation** [Evidence Hub: What drives health inequalities?](https://health.org.uk/evidence-hub?msclkid=9c4fec7fab7d11eca7baa68b38534b40), providing data, insights and analysis exploring how the circumstances in which we live shape our health.

**World Health Organization** [Health Equity Monitor, Health Equity Assessment Toolkit, and Health Equity Country Profiles](https://www.who.int/data/health-equity), supporting Member states to strengthen their health information systems to collect, analyse, report and use inequality data. 

**World Health Organization** [Health Equity Status Report Initiative Health Equity Dataset](https://worldhealthorg.shinyapps.io/european_health_equity_dataset/), an interactive platform based on the [Health Equity Assessment Toolkit Plus (HEAT Plus)](https://www.who.int/data/health-equity/assessment_toolkit).
