**The policy indicators and their link to health equity (including the data indicators they align with):**

**1. Relative Poverty**
This indicator measures the number of people living on or below 60% of the median household
disposable income after taxes and social transfers. It is a measure of the proportion of the
population exposed to income insecurity and consequently at risk of poor health.
**2. Social Assistance Coverage**
This indicator measures the extent to which social assistance reduces financial insecurity
among those with the fewest resources and supports people to access the resources and
services needed to live a healthy life. Non-contributory social support such as conditional and
unconditional social transfers, school meals, public works programmes, social pensions and
fee waivers reduce the risk of financial hardship, for example due to housing, fuel, and food
insecurity, and the consequent physical and mental health risks.
