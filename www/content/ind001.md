## Adult self-reported health
the percentage of surveyed adults reporting poor or fair health is a combined measure with data taken from three surveys: the European Union Statistics on Income and Living Conditions (EU-SILC) Survey, The European Social Survey (ESS) and The World Values Survey (WVS).
Disaggregation: INCOME, EDUCATION


### Question related to health status.
Surveyed people were asked to report on their health. The EU-SILC survey question is: How is your health in general? The original response options were - Very bad, bad, fair, good, very good. These options were re-coded by HESR analysts to form a binary variable as follows - Very bad, bad or fair versus good or very good.

The ESS survey question is: How is your health in general? The original response options were - Very bad, bad, fair, good, very good. These options were re-coded by HESR analysts to form a binary variable as follows - Very bad, bad or fair versus good or very good.
The WVS survey question is: All in all, how would you describe your state of health these days? The original response options were - Very poor, poor, fair, good, very good. These options were re-coded by HESR analysts to form a binary variable as follows - Very poor, poor or fair versus good or very good.

### Question related to income.
Surveyed people were asked to report on their income.
The EU-SILC survey participants were asked to report on their total disposable household income. The original response options were re-coded by the data owners into the equivalised disposable household income. HESR analysts calculated quintiles of the equivalised disposable household income distribution per country per year, adjusted using survey weights.
The ESS survey participants were asked to report on their household's total income, after tax and compulsory deductions, from all sources. The original response options were re-coded by the data owners into deciles of the actual household income range for each country. These options were re-coded by HESR analysts into income quintiles. The indicator was derived by calculating survey weighted averages of the data by country, year, sex, age, education and income quintile (where available). Cells based on samples of 50 survey participants have been suppressed. Rates for the total population (those aged 16+ years) are directly age standardized with the World Health Organization European standard population. Data for those aged 16-64 and 65+ years are presented as crude rates. Only EU-SILC and ESS data are included in the income-disaggregated indicator. Data from all three sources are included in the education-disaggregated indicator (please see separate measure notes).

### Question related to level of education
Surveyed people were asked to report on their highest level of education.
The EU-SILC survey participants were asked to report on the highest education level they had attained. The original response options were - Before 2014: 0 'pre-primary education', 1 'primary education', 2 'lower secondary education', 3 ' (upper) secondary education', 4 'post-secondary non tertiary education', 5 'first stage of tertiary education (not leading directly to an advanced research qualification)', 6 'second stage of tertiary education (leading to an advanced research qualification)'. From 2014 onwards: 0 'Less than primary education', 1 'Primary education', 2 'Lower secondary education', 3 'Upper secondary education', 4 'Post-secondary non-tertiary education', 5 'Short cycle tertiary', 6 'Bachelor or equivalent', 7 'Master or equivalent', 8 'Doctorate or equivalent'. These options were re-coded by HESR analysts to form a three category variable as follows - 1) low level education - pre-primary to lower secondary education only, 2) medium level education - upper secondary to post-secondary non-tertiary education, and 3) high level education - tertiary education (original options 0-2; 3-4; >4, respectively).
The ESS survey question is: What is the highest level of education you have achieved? The original response options were re-coded by the data owners into the ESS Education Detailed ISCED Coding Frame - 1 'ES-ISCED I , less than lower secondary', 2 'ES-ISCED II, lower secondary', 3 'ES-ISCED IIIb, lower tier upper secondary', 4 'ES-ISCED IIIa, upper tier upper secondary', 5 'ES-ISCED IV, advanced vocational, sub-degree', 6 'ES-ISCED V1, lower tertiary education, BA level', 7 'ES-ISCED V2, higher tertiary education, > MA level'. These options were re-coded by HESR analysts to form a three category variable as follows - 1) low level education - pre-primary to lower secondary education only, 2) medium level education - upper secondary to post-secondary non-tertiary education, and 3) high level education - tertiary education (original options 1-2; 3-5; 6-7, respectively).
The WVS survey question is: What is the highest educational level that you have attained? The original response options were - 1 'Inadequately completed elementary education', 2 'Completed (compulsory) elementary education', 3 'Incomplete secondary school: technical/vocational type/(Compulsory) elementary education and basic vocational qualification', 4 'Complete secondary school: technical/vocational type/Secondary, intermediate vocational qualification', 5 'Incomplete secondary: university-preparatory type/Secondary, intermediate general qualification', 6 'Complete secondary: university-preparatory type/Full secondary, maturity level certificate', 7 'Some university without degree/Higher education - lower-level tertiary certificate', 8 'University with degree/Higher education - upper-level tertiary certificate'. These options were re-coded by HESR analysts to form a three category variable as follows - 1) low level education - pre-primary to lower secondary education only, 2) medium level education - upper secondary to post-secondary non-tertiary education, and 3) high level education - tertiary education (original options 1-4; 5-6; 7-8, respectively).

These categories correspond to the classification of the International Standard Classification of Education (ISCED) version of 2011, grouped into three categories as follows: Pre-primary, primary and lower secondary education:
- Level 0: no formal education or below primary education
- Level 1: Primary education or first stage of basic education
- Level 2: Lower secondary or second stage of basic education
Upper secondary and post-secondary non-tertiary education:
- Level 3: Upper secondary education
- Level 4: Post-secondary non-tertiary education
First and second stage of tertiary education:
- Level 5: First stage of tertiary education
- Level 6: Second stage of tertiary education.
The indicator was derived by calculating survey weighted averages of the data by country, year, sex, age, and education. Cells based on samples of 50 survey participants have been suppressed. Rates for the total population (those aged 16+ years) are directly age standardized with the World Health Organization European standard population. Data for those aged 16-64 and 65+ years are presented as crude rates. For data sourced from WVS the year presented is the wave midrange by country. Only EU-SILC and ESS data are included in the income-disaggregated indicator. Data from all three sources are included in the education-disaggregated indicator (please see separate measure notes).


### Country notes
Most recent year of data available: 2016 or 2017 (for a small number of countries only earlier data from 2005 to 2015 are available).
